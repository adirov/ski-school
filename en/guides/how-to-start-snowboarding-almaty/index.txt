22:Tdbe,<h2 id="in-short">In short</h2>
<p>Start with the basics rather than trying to turn straight away. On day one a beginner learns to stand on the board, stop, and slide down sideways (the &quot;falling leaf&quot;). Linked turns come later, at a different pace for everyone. Learning with an instructor is easier: they correct your stance right away and show you how to fall safely.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-choose-how-to-learn">Step 1. Choose how to learn</h2>
<div class="table-wrap"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Format</th><th scope="col">Who it suits</th><th scope="col">What to keep in mind</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Private lesson</td><td>People who want to learn faster or are nervous about falling</td><td>The instructor focuses only on you, but it costs more</td></tr><tr><td>Group course</td><td>People learning with friends or who enjoy company</td><td>The group should be at the same level</td></tr><tr><td>On your own</td><td>People with ski or skateboard experience</td><td>Higher risk of bad habits and injury</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>Almaty Snowboard School offers private lessons, group courses and kids training; we share durations and prices on request. Ask us on the <a href="/en/contact/">contact page</a>.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-sort-out-equipment">Step 2. Sort out equipment</h2>
<p>There is no need to buy a board before your first lessons. A soft, short rental board suits beginners. Boots should hold your heel firmly without squeezing your toes. A helmet is a must; impact shorts and knee pads make early falls much softer.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-pick-the-day-and-the-slope">Step 3. Pick the day and the slope</h2>
<p>A first lesson needs a gentle, quiet learning area. Weekdays are usually calmer than weekends. Before you go, check:</p>
<ul>
<li>that the lift to the learning slope is running;</li>
<li>the weather forecast and visibility;</li>
<li>current lift pass prices on the resort&#39;s website.</li>
</ul>
<p>We do not publish resort schedules or prices because they change. Check the official website on the day.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-find-your-stance">Step 4. Find your stance</h2>
<p>&quot;Regular&quot; means left foot forward, &quot;goofy&quot; means right foot forward. A quick test: stand still and ask someone to give you a gentle push from behind. The foot you step forward with is probably your front foot. Your instructor will confirm it on the slope.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-first-drills">Step 5. First drills</h2>
<p>A typical first lesson goes like this:</p>
<ol>
<li>Walking and climbing with one foot strapped in.</li>
<li>Gliding straight on an almost flat area.</li>
<li>Stopping on an edge and sliding down on the heel and toe edges.</li>
<li>The &quot;falling leaf&quot;: zigzagging down without switching edges.</li>
</ol>
<p>If you can stop confidently on both edges by the end of the day, that is a good result.</p>
<h2 id="common-beginner-mistakes">Common beginner mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Looking at your feet instead of where you are going.</li>
<li>Leaning back away from the slope, which shoots the board forward.</li>
<li>Putting your hands out when you fall. It is safer to land on your forearms or bottom with your chin tucked.</li>
<li>Riding until you are exhausted. Tired legs make more mistakes, so it is better to stop early.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-next">What next</h2>
<p>After a basic lesson you can continue privately or in a group. If you would like us to suggest a format for your dates and level, <a href="/en/contact/">send a request</a>.</p>
