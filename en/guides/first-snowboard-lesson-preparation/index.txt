23:Td43,<h2 id="in-short">In short</h2>
<p>Your first lesson goes more smoothly when your body is ready for the effort, your clothes stay dry and your equipment is sorted before the lesson starts. Here is a day-by-day plan.</p>
<h2 id="a-week-before-get-your-body-ready">A week before: get your body ready</h2>
<p>Snowboarding works your thighs, glutes, core and ankles. If you have not exercised for a while, spend 10–15 minutes a day for 5–7 days before the trip on:</p>
<ul>
<li>squats and lunges;</li>
<li>planks on straight arms and on forearms;</li>
<li>standing on one leg with your eyes closed;</li>
<li>a light hamstring stretch.</li>
</ul>
<p>The goal is not to build muscle but to keep your legs from tiring out in the first hour.</p>
<h2 id="the-evening-before-clothes-and-documents">The evening before: clothes and documents</h2>
<p>Pack in layers: a thermal base layer, a warm mid layer and a waterproof jacket and trousers. Avoid cotton: it gets wet and makes you cold. Bring a spare pair of gloves or mittens.</p>
<p>Check that you have:</p>
<ul>
<li>an ID document (rental shops may ask for one);</li>
<li>a charger or power bank: phones drain faster in the cold;</li>
<li>sunscreen and lip balm;</li>
<li>ski goggles or sunglasses.</li>
</ul>
<p>The full list is in our article <a href="/en/guides/what-to-bring-to-the-mountains-almaty/">&quot;What to bring to the mountains of Almaty&quot;</a>.</p>
<h2 id="in-the-morning-breakfast-and-time">In the morning: breakfast and time</h2>
<p>Eat a proper breakfast: you burn energy faster in the cold and at altitude. Bring water and a snack. Leave enough time for the journey, the rental queue and changing, so you do not start the lesson tired and stressed.</p>
<h2 id="rental-how-to-tell-the-gear-fits">Rental: how to tell the gear fits</h2>
<div class="table-wrap"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">What to check</th><th scope="col">How it should be</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Boots</td><td>Your heel does not lift when you lean forward; toes lightly touch the front</td></tr><tr><td>Board length</td><td>For a beginner, usually between chin and nose height</td></tr><tr><td>Bindings</td><td>They close without force and the straps are not twisted</td></tr><tr><td>Helmet</td><td>It does not wobble when you tilt your head and does not press</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>If something rubs or hurts, say so at the rental shop straight away, not an hour later on the slope.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-tell-your-instructor">What to tell your instructor</h2>
<p>A good instructor will ask, but it helps to mention up front:</p>
<ul>
<li>whether you have tried snowboarding, skiing, skateboarding or surfing;</li>
<li>any knee, back or wrist injuries;</li>
<li>what scares you: speed, lifts, falling;</li>
<li>what you want to achieve by the end of the day.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="during-the-lesson">During the lesson</h2>
<p>Drink water, take short breaks and say honestly when you are tired. Mistakes and falls on day one are normal. Getting your stopping right matters more than rushing into turns.</p>
<h2 id="after-the-lesson">After the lesson</h2>
<p>Do a light stretch in the evening and get enough sleep. If you plan a second day in a row, expect sore muscles. Do not ignore sharp joint pain or a blow to the head: see a doctor.</p>
