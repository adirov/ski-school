## In short

You can learn to snowboard at any age if you start with the right basics. On day one a beginner learns to stand on the board, stop, and slide down sideways (the "falling leaf"). Linked turns come later, at a different pace for everyone. It is best to learn with an instructor: self-taught attempts often end in falls onto the back and wrists.

## Step 1. Choose how to learn

| Format | Who it suits | What to keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Private lesson | People who want to learn faster or are nervous about falling | The instructor focuses only on you, but it costs more |
| Group course | People learning with friends or who enjoy company | The group should be at the same level |
| On your own | People with ski or skateboard experience | Higher risk of bad habits and injury |

Almaty Snowboard School offers private lessons, group courses and kids training; we share durations and prices on request. Ask us on the [contact page](/en/contact/).

## Step 2. Sort out equipment

There is no need to buy a board before your first lessons. A soft, short rental board suits beginners. Boots should hold your heel firmly without squeezing your toes. A helmet is a must; impact shorts and knee pads make early falls much softer.

## Step 3. Pick the day and the slope

A first lesson needs a gentle, quiet learning area. Weekdays are usually calmer than weekends. Before you go, check:

- that the lift to the learning slope is running;
- the weather forecast and visibility;
- current lift pass prices on the resort's website.

We do not publish resort schedules or prices because they change. Check the official website on the day.

## Step 4. Find your stance

"Regular" means left foot forward, "goofy" means right foot forward. A quick test: stand still and ask someone to give you a gentle push from behind. The foot you step forward with is probably your front foot. Your instructor will confirm it on the slope.

## Step 5. First drills

A typical first lesson goes like this:

1. Walking and climbing with one foot strapped in.
2. Gliding straight on an almost flat area.
3. Stopping on an edge and sliding down on the heel and toe edges.
4. The "falling leaf": zigzagging down without switching edges.

If you can stop confidently on both edges by the end of the day, that is a good result.

## Common beginner mistakes

- Looking at your feet instead of where you are going.
- Leaning back away from the slope, which shoots the board forward.
- Putting your hands out when you fall. It is safer to land on your forearms or bottom with your chin tucked.
- Riding until you are exhausted. Most injuries happen at the end of the day.
